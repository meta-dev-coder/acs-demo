How the Lane Closure Revenue Optimizer works

This demo walks one real maintenance decision from trigger to schedule: a lane has to close for repair work, and the twin ranks the closure windows so the planner can pick the one that loses the least revenue without adding risk.

“When do we close the lane so it costs the least money, stays inside lane-availability commitments, and doesn’t cause the next crash?”

·How to start

From the demo launcher, open the UC1 · Lane Closure Revenue Optimizer tile. The twin opens on a full-screen startup card — press the one button to begin.

the “ⓘ” next to any number in the ranked table A table of your candidate windows ranked by revenue loss, delay, throughput, lane availability and secondary-crash risk — cheapest safe window highlighted. The ⓘ opens every number's ingredients, so nothing is a black box.

Decide
“Schedule this window” on the winning row The decision is logged with the evidence that produced it, a short SUMO closure animation plays on the twin, and the corridor-level exec KPI strip updates to reflect the new decision.

∑The formulas behind the ranking

D(s, t) = demand, in vehicles per 15‑minute interval, for segment s at time t
How many drivers want this stretch of road before any closure changes it — the baseline everything else is measured against.
Cclosed = 1600 × open lanes × 0.9 (merge friction)
Remaining road capacity once the closure narrows the lanes: 1,600 vehicles/hour per open lane, discounted 10% for the friction of merging around the work zone.
R(w) = ∑t∈w [diverted(t) + suppressed(t)] × toll rate
Dollars of toll revenue this window forfeits — trips that reroute around the closure, or simply don't happen, valued at the toll.
E(w) = historical closure‑incident rate × exposure in w
Safety exposure for this window: this segment's own crash-during-closure history (blended with corridor-wide history where the segment's own record is thin), scaled by how much traffic sits in that window.
Score(w) = w1·R + w2·delay + w3·E + w4·crew cost
The single ranking number: a weighted blend of revenue loss, driver delay, safety exposure and crew cost. Lowest score wins — the "cheapest safe window," not just the cheapest one.

?Why trust this?

Backtest

The model is trained on year 1 of two years of real ACS incident history and asked to predict year 2 blind, then graded against what actually happened — it never sees the answer before predicting it.

Editable assumptions

Every input behind a run — demand curve, toll rate, weights — is visible and editable live. Change a number and the ranked table re-ranks in front of you: a real stress-test, not a canned demo.

Track record

Every scheduled decision is logged and rolled into the corridor-level exec KPI strip, so the system's own accuracy becomes a running scoreboard rather than a one-time claim.

Honest about the line

"You shouldn't trust us — you should check us." Real data drives the parts that can be checked; the parts that can't yet are labeled plainly, not hidden.
