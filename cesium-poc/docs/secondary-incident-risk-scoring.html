Secondary Incident Risk

How the 0–100 score is calculated, what each band means, and a worked example of a High-risk incident.

I-595 Corridor Digital Twin · Traffic Management Center · Source of truth: src/tmc/secondaryIncidentRisk.js and src/liveOps/operationalImpact.js. Worked example measured from the running application, not reconstructed.

What this score is, and is not It is a rule-based indicator built from fields that exist in the connected data. It is not a prediction and not a probability: nothing here has been validated against actual secondary-crash outcomes. It answers “given this incident and what is around it, how much evidence is there of elevated secondary risk?” — a property of the incident. That is a different question from Operational Impact, which measures disruption on a road section. Impact is one input here, never the output.

1 · The bands

The score is a plain sum of factor contributions — no normalisation, no division. The band is simply where that sum lands.

BandScoreReading
Severe90 – 100Multiple heavy factors firing together
High60 – 89Strong evidence of elevated secondary risk
Moderate30 – 59Some contributing conditions
Low0 – 29Little evidence from what the data shows

A single blocked lane (18) on its own deliberately does not reach Moderate.

Why these weights, in three sentences Lane blockage leads because it is both the best-evidenced field in this feed and the factor most directly implicated in secondary crashes — traffic stopping where drivers do not expect it. Duration is capped at 16 so that a long-running incident can never outvote a blocked lane; it is a worse exposure but weak evidence by comparison. Location history is capped at 18 and weighted down for low spatial confidence, because the register’s coordinates are derived from damaged assets rather than surveyed crash geometry.

“0” and “Not evaluated” are different statements Environment scored 0 because the weather was measured and found benign. Location history says Not evaluated because fewer than 3 register records lie within the search radius — below the floor at which a concentration ratio is stable enough to band. The interface keeps these apart deliberately: a component scoring zero for want of data is not a reassurance.

4 · How Operational Impact HIGH is derived

This comes from a different model — Live Ops’ operational impact — and describes the road section, not the incident. Every event on the section scores base + severity + lanes, and the section’s scores are summed.

For section I595-WB-FDOT-006680-007350, this incident alone contributes 40 (incident base) + 40 (Major) + 50 (2 lanes × 25) = 130, comfortably past the 80 threshold for HIGH.

Why the lane weight differs between the two models Section impact uses 25 points per blocked lane; secondary risk uses 18 and caps at two lanes. They answer different questions — “how disrupted is this road” versus “how likely is another crash here” — so they are weighted independently. Operational Impact enters the secondary-risk score as one input worth at most 24 of 100, and never as the score itself.

5 · Data confidence — a separate axis

Confidence measures how much of the picture was available. It is reported beside the score and never discounts it: a High score stands on the evidence that produced it.

confidence = evaluated ÷ (evaluated + unknown + 4 permanently unavailable)  ·  High ≥ 0.60, Medium ≥ 0.35, otherwise Low.

The four permanently unavailable factors — measured as present in 0 of 61 live events — are responder status, DMS message content, queue length and traffic speed or delay. They are declared on every assessment so that a low score is never mistaken for a quiet corridor.

6 · How “Recommended attention” is ranked

The list is not re-derived from the score. Each item comes from a factor that actually fired, and carries a fixed priority.

PriorityBases that produce it
HIGHLane closure · upstream congestion · weather · upstream unresolved
MEDIUMMonitoring · Operational Impact · incident duration · historical location
LOWUnavailable data — stated as gaps, not as actions

Sorted by priority, stable within each priority. One escalation rule applies: if upstream could not be resolved, duration is promoted to HIGH — a long-running incident whose approach you cannot see is worse than one you can. In the worked example upstream was resolved (camera 5036), so duration stayed MEDIUM in fourth place.

Items marked “Data gap — this is what to establish, not an action already available” are ranked LOW on purpose: they tell an operator what they cannot see, rather than offering an action the system cannot actually support.

Three rules the model will not break Missing data is never treated as zero risk. A record-frequency comparison is never called a crash rate. An asset-derived coordinate is never called validated crash geometry.
