# Ask the Twin answers one silo, not two

Ask the Twin is about **halfway to Dan's scenario**. The de-siloed half is real. The `server/ask/` orchestrator already answers across maintenance, safety and TMC data: assets, tickets, work orders, inspections, crashes, live FL511 events and weather. The real snapshot data even contains the example: **exactly three camera sites with open work orders**, opened 2024-08-21, 2025-01-17 and 2025-05-07, so a first-in-first-out ordering exists today. The stakeholder half is missing. There are no data-source toggles: every request gets the same tools and the same data, and personas change only the wording. **No finance, tolling or transaction data is reachable**, and no tool ranks sites by switchable criteria. Two facts change how the executive story should be told. First, FDOT, not the concessionaire, operates the I-595 Express tolling equipment and **keeps the toll revenue**, so "revenue at risk" belongs to FDOT, while the concessionaire's exposure is availability-payment deductions. Second, the three sites in the data are **CCTV traffic cameras, not toll plate cameras**, and they sit on one traffic segment 3–4 km from the nearest gantry. A revenue lens that reorders them therefore needs authored, clearly labelled synthetic data. The fix fits the existing architecture: a scope field in the request envelope, scope enforced on the server, one `prioritize_sites` insight that does its joins in code, and a synthetic per-site revenue source. I estimate **roughly 8–13 engineering days** for a credible demo, with real revenue data depending on FDOT/FTE data access that this repo does not have.

## The de-siloed half exists; the stakeholder half does not

A note on which backend this report covers. CLAUDE.md says Vite does not mount `/api/i595/ask`, but that is now out of date. `vite.config.js:45` loads `server/ask/localAsk.mjs`, and `src/askTheTwin.js:21-24` sends dev builds to `/api/i595/ask`. Production builds still default to the CloudFront Lambda. Everything below is about the `server/ask/` orchestrator ([vite.config.js](../cesium-poc/vite.config.js), [askTheTwin.js](../cesium-poc/src/askTheTwin.js)).

The orchestrator reads from three kinds of source. The first is a SQLite twin store holding eight historical DataConnect classes, plus the six SDNA live classes when DataConnect is reachable ([twinStore.mjs:36-53](../cesium-poc/server/ask/twinStore.mjs), [dataConnectPull.mjs:108-178](../cesium-poc/server/ask/dataConnectPull.mjs)). The second is six corridor GeoJSON layers. The third is live FL511 events and Open-Meteo weather. On top of these sit seven read tools and four insight tools: incident ranking and explanation for the TMC, crash hotspots and weather risk for safety ([tools.mjs:36-93](../cesium-poc/server/ask/tools.mjs), [insights.mjs:318-340](../cesium-poc/server/ask/insights.mjs)). That covers maintenance, safety and TMC well. **Finance and tolling are not covered at all.** The only volume figure the model can see is FDOT's 2025 AADT, one annual value per traffic segment. The insights themselves say so: "Traffic volume and speed at the time are not in the twin" ([insights.mjs:145](../cesium-poc/server/ask/insights.mjs)). Even the maintenance cost signal is empty: `Total Cost` is null on all 854 work orders ([work_orders.json](../cesium-poc/public/dataconnect-data/work_orders.json)).

The scoping gap is stated in the code itself. The persona comment reads "A persona picks the playbook and the starter questions; it never grants tools or data — every session gets the same read-only tool set until real sign-in exists" ([envelope.mjs:11-15](../cesium-poc/server/ask/envelope.mjs)). The settings panel tells users the same thing ([askTheTwin.js:138](../cesium-poc/src/askTheTwin.js)). Map layer visibility (`mapLayerStore`) is never sent with a question. The screen snapshot carries the workspace, persona, temporal mode, selection, view bbox and drawn area ([askTheTwin.js:1207-1217](../cesium-poc/src/askTheTwin.js)). The server sanitiser drops any other key, so a `sources` field sent today would be thrown away ([envelope.mjs:53-72](../cesium-poc/server/ask/envelope.mjs)). The tool definitions are built once and passed unchanged on every model call ([askService.mjs:42-45](../cesium-poc/server/ask/askService.mjs), [orchestrator.mjs:322,335](../cesium-poc/server/ask/orchestrator.mjs)). The three personas are tmc_operator, maintenance_manager and safety_engineer. There is no executive persona.

Revenue and transaction logic does exist in the repo, but **all of it is synthetic and outside Ask the Twin's reach**. UC1's Lane Closure Revenue Optimizer uses a self-described "synthetic demand curve" and a `tollRateUsd: 2.5`. Its weighted Score(w) uses revenue 0.5, delay 0.2, safety 0.3 and crew 0, with weights editable through sliders ([windowConfig.json](../cesium-poc/config/windowConfig.json), [windowEval.js:211-224](../cesium-poc/src/windowEval.js), [trustPanel.js:25-31](../cesium-poc/src/trustPanel.js)). The NTTA/DNT demo has the closest analogue to Dan's idea: in `assetOps.js`, camera degradation raises the missed-read rate, and missed reads times a $1.45 average toll give revenue risk per hour. The root iTwin app's Scenario C dynamic pricing runs on stubs and, per CLAUDE.md, never imports cesium-poc. The code is honest about all this: "Delay and revenue are explicitly OUT of scope here — there are no traffic actuals in this package" ([backtest.js:19-27](../cesium-poc/src/backtest.js)). The 15 seeded decisions carrying `revenueAtRiskUsd` are flagged `"seeded": true` ([decisions_seed.json](../cesium-poc/public/dataconnect-data/decisions_seed.json)).

The maintenance side of the demo runs on real snapshot data. Of 13 camera work orders, three are not closed: **WO-900590 (CCTV 5078, Open since 2024-08-21)**, **WO-900717 (CCTV 5075, In Progress since 2025-01-17)** and **WO-900775 (CCTV 5079, Awaiting Parts since 2025-05-07)**. FIFO order is therefore 5078 → 5075 → 5079. Two caveats apply. None of the three work orders is a replacement; their types are preventive maintenance and root-cause investigation. And camera identifiers live in three systems that do not join cleanly. Work orders use the registry's numeric `Asset ID`, ITS inspections use the description string ("CCTV 5078"), and the 74 FL511 map cameras use FL511 `camera_id`s that match neither.

## Shipped products scope by role preset, by map, and by per-question chip

No product surveyed does exactly what Dan describes, but three shipped patterns together cover it. **Per-question pickers** let end users choose sources before a run. Gemini Deep Research adds Gmail, Drive and Chat alongside Search, which is on by default, and selecting a source makes it available without forcing its use ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/gemini-deep-research-can-now-connect-to-your-gmail-docs-drive-and-even-chat); [MacMyths](https://macmyths.com/gemini-ai-can-read-your-gmail-now-but-its-not-reading-every-inbox/)). Notion AI has a source picker over its connectors ([Notion Help](https://www.notion.com/help/guides/everything-you-can-do-with-notion-ai)). Microsoft 365 Copilot shipped Work/Web tabs and then replaced them with a "Work IQ" control in a 2026 redesign; sources give conflicting dates for that change ([MC1325422](https://mc.merill.net/message/MC1325422)). **Builder-time scopes** are stored presets. Atlassian Rovo agents can be restricted with "Only reference these sources" but remain subject to the user's permissions ([Atlassian](https://support.atlassian.com/rovo/docs/knowledge-sources-for-agents)). Databricks Genie spaces curate a small table set and label answers that come from "trusted assets", showing their parameter values ([Databricks](https://docs.databricks.com/genie/trusted-assets)). Palantir AIP passes a filtered object set from application state into an agent's retrieval context and allow-lists which properties reach the LLM ([Palantir](https://palantir.com/docs/foundry/agent-studio/retrieval-context)). **Map-scoped assistants** treat the map as the scope. Esri's Data Explorer answers only about "the data in the map", with no external sources ([Esri](https://www.esri.com/arcgis-blog/products/instant-apps/geoai/introducing-data-explorer)).

For Ask the Twin, these map onto a clear design. Persona presets play the role of Genie spaces or Rovo agents. Source chips mirror the map's layer list, with the visible layers in `mapLayerStore` as a sensible default, which goes one step past Esri by allowing a per-question override. Every answer then states its effective scope. The documented pitfalls show where this goes wrong. Microsoft's Agent Builder shows the web toggle as on while tenant policy blocks it, which Microsoft calls "a UI limitation" ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/copilot/extensibility/agent-builder)). A Palantir agent failed to refresh its object-set context after the initial load ([Palantir Community](https://community.palantir.com/t/aip-agent-studio-doesnt-update-object-set-context/1545)). Rovo users report scope leakage, which the official documentation disputes ([Atlassian Community](https://community.atlassian.com/forums/Confluence-questions/Rovo-agents/qaa-p/3079592)). One finding raises the stakes on all of this: in a large randomized experiment, reference links **increased trust in AI search answers even when the links were wrong** ([arXiv 2504.06435](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.06435)). The cue that matters most is therefore the *exclusion* line ("revenue data is toggled off; including it could change the ranking"), not the citation list. I found no shipped product that offers a "compare with vs. without source X" answer, so a rank-delta view would be new.

The architecture lesson is to **treat focus as a filter applied on top of permissions, never as a replacement for them, and to enforce it on the server**. Anthropic's prompt cache is ordered tools → system → messages, and "modifying tool definitions… invalidates the entire cache" ([Anthropic: Prompt caching](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching)). With about 11 tools, well below the 30–50 tools at which tool-selection accuracy degrades ([Anthropic: Tool search](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/tool-use/tool-search-tool)), the cheapest correct design keeps the `tools` array and system prompt constant. The per-question scope goes in the user turn, and `tools.run` rejects out-of-scope classes with a typed `out_of_scope` gap. This differs from the obvious edit of filtering `tools.definitions` per request and keying the cached prompt by persona plus sources. That edit would split the cache into up to 3 × 2⁴ variants and still rely on the model to respect the scope. Databricks gives the counterweight: unnecessary tables cause "confusing or incorrect answers" ([Databricks](https://docs.databricks.com/aws/en/genie/set-up)). Stating the scope in the turn, and having `describe_store` list excluded classes as excluded, gets most of that focus benefit without breaking the cache.

## FIFO, distance and revenue are weights; SLAs and safety are pins

Switchable criteria are credible because they follow established practice rather than invent it. Under MAP-21, state TAMPs must be **risk-based**. FHWA guidance says a risk-based plan can let a low-risk asset fall below target in order to address a higher-risk structure or traffic-control device ([FHWA 2017](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/asset/pubs/incorporating_rm.pdf)). ITS practice ranks devices by how critical they are to operations, with safety and traffic-control devices above traveler information ([ITS KRS](https://www.itskrs.its.dot.gov/2006-l00308); [TxDOT 0-6773](https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/37629)). Commercial tools use the shape the feature needs. IBM Maximo combines asset/location priority with work-order priority into a configurable `CALCPRIORITY`, for example `PRIORITY + 2*EQPRIORITY`, which drives a respond-by date ([IBM](https://www.ibm.com/support/pages/node/881486); [IBM APAR](https://www.ibm.com/support/pages/apar/IZ85044)). Salesforce Field Service separates pass/fail **work rules** from **service objectives** "weighted from most to least important", such as minimizing travel ([Trailhead](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/field-service-lightning-scheduling-basics/examine-scheduling-policies)). The MCDA literature supports giving each stakeholder its own weight vector over the same data, then testing whether rankings survive weight changes ([MDPI Sustainability](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/15/20/14953); [Frontiers 2019](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/built-environment/articles/10.3389/fbuil.2019.00006/epub)). NCHRP 806 frames cross-asset allocation as scenario comparison ([TransportationOps](https://dev.transportationops.org/news/research/guide-cross-asset-resource-allocation-and-impact-transportation-system-performance)).

The recommended scoring model has two layers. **Pins** are hard constraints shown above the ranked list under every preset: an SLA breach within the window, a safety-critical failure, or a camera covering an active incident. Toll contracts show why pins are needed. NCTA requires **on-site repair response within 2 hours** for emergency repairs ([NCTA RFP](https://connect.ncdot.gov/business/Turnpike/ProcurementsLibrary/RTCS_RFP_Attach19.pdf)), and CTRMA enforces Kapsch maintenance KPIs through liquidated damages ([CTRMA AIS](https://mobilityauthority.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/13_1_FINAL_AIS_Kapsch_Settlement.pdf)). An executive preset must never be able to bury those. Below the pins is a **weighted sum over normalized criteria**: queue age (FIFO), travel distance from a depot, inspection risk and work-order priority, safety exposure, and revenue at risk. Presets are named weight vectors, visible and editable. Weighted sum beats TOPSIS or AHP at runtime because each criterion's contribution can be drawn as a bar. TOPSIS can also reorder results when alternatives enter or leave a changing queue. AHP still has a use offline, for deriving preset weights from stakeholder pairwise comparisons. **Route batching** (visiting nearby sites in one trip) belongs to dispatch after ranking, not inside the score. Weights should sit in config next to `scoreWindow`'s, not in code.

The output should show three things UC1's trust panel already hints at. The first is a rank-delta view on preset switch ("CCTV 5079 moved from #3 to #1"). The second is the per-criterion contribution behind each move. The third is a stability flag: "top 3 identical under all presets" when that holds, and a warning when a site moves more than N places under ±10–20% weight perturbation. Missing inputs must show as `NA` and go in the insight's existing `notEvaluated` list, with weights renormalized and the gap stated. They must never be imputed. Distance with no depot configured, and revenue with finance toggled off, are both cases of this. This matches the codebase rule and the prompt's own instruction to "never rank incidents or estimate risk yourself" ([prompt.mjs:18](../cesium-poc/server/ask/prompt.mjs)). The ranking must be computed by a tool, never by the LLM chaining a four-hop join across 50-row results inside six rounds. One caveat on positioning: I found **no published example of revenue-weighted maintenance prioritization in tolling or ITS**. The feature should be described as applying risk-based TAM and MCDA practice to the revenue consequence, not as copying an agency's method.

## FDOT loses the toll revenue, and CCTV cameras don't collect it

The I-595 Express facts change who the "executive" is. It is a 35-year DBFOM availability-payment P3 with I-595 Express, LLC (ACS Infrastructure). **FDOT "installed, tested, operates and maintains all tolling equipment… and sets the toll rates and retains the toll revenue"**, while the concessionaire receives performance-based monthly availability payments, capped at **$65.9M (2009 dollars)** a year and escalating ([FHWA project profile](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/ipd/project_profiles/fl_i595.aspx)). Those payments fall when performance or roadway-availability requirements are missed ([USDOT Build America](https://www.transportation.gov/buildamerica/projects/i-595-corridor-roadway-improvements)). Florida's availability-payment model charges more for closures in peak periods ([World Highways](https://worldhighways.com/wh8/wh10/feature/how-florida-paved-way-availability-payments-us)). That gives two honest executive lenses. **FDOT's lens is toll revenue and enforcement.** **The concessionaire's lens is availability deductions and noncompliance points**, like those FDOT issued on I-4 Ultimate ([Construction Dive](https://www.constructiondive.com/news/fdot-p3-nix-deadline-for-i-4-100m-overrun-delay-claims/542908/)). That second lens applies only if the failed asset is in the concessionaire's O&M scope, which I could not verify. A demo that says "the executive sees revenue impact" without naming whose revenue will not survive a knowledgeable FDOT or ACS audience.

The second fact is technical. Toll revenue depends on gantry plate cameras and transponder readers, not on CCTV. When a plate camera fails, transponder trips still post, but image-dependent trips are lost for the outage window: violations, and tag misreads that would otherwise be posted by plate ([USPTO 10019640](https://patents.justia.com/patent/10019640)). On I-595 that share is small. The 595 Express FAQ says the lanes are SunPass-only, two-axle, with **no Toll-by-Plate** and a **$0.50** minimum ([595 Express FAQ](https://www.595express.info/faq.shtm)). Violators face the toll plus **$25/day and a $2.50 administrative fee**, as on 95 Express ([95 Express](https://95express.com/?p=908)). A March 2026 article implies Toll-By-Plate billing, so the policy may have changed; this is unconfirmed. The three sites in the data are SunGuide-style **CCTV**, whose outage costs incident-detection capability, not transactions. To keep the scenario honest, the demo has two options. It can switch the asset class to toll-gantry plate cameras by seeding an asset type that does not exist in the registry today. Or it can model CCTV revenue impact indirectly, as slower incident detection leading to longer express-lane closures and lost tolls or availability deductions. Either way, AADT will not separate the three CCTV sites: all three lie 24–47 m from the same FDOT segment (AADT 229,000), and all are 3.2–3.9 km from gantry GNT-M05. **Any reordering by revenue will be authored, not discovered**, and has to be disclosed.

The best public model for the data shape is NY MTA's hourly reconciled crossings dataset. It is broken down by facility and payment method, including an explicit **unbillable** category, across about 21.7M rows ([data.gov](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/mta-bridges-tunnels-hourly-reconciled-crossings-2019-2024)). TBTA's technical and business reason codes for rejected images supply a disposition field ([NY OSC audit](https://www.osc.ny.gov/files/state-agencies/audits/pdf/sga-2022-21f15.pdf)). A synthetic `toll_site_hourly` source should copy that shape: site, direction (including reversible mode), hour, transponder, image and unbillable counts with a reason, average toll, camera status, and a `synthetic: true` flag. Revenue at risk per site is tx/hour × image-dependent share × (toll + expected fee recovery) × outage hours × unrecoverable fraction, plus any contract penalty. Show it with ranges, not single values: image share 2–25% (SunPass-only lanes at the low end) and an unrecoverable fraction of 0.7–1.0 during a full outage. The research notes include a worked example: three assumed sites at ~$6,480, ~$1,013 and ~$4,500 a month, where condition-first ranking puts the low-volume site first and revenue ranking reverses it. Every input in that example is invented, and the demo must badge it as such using the codebase's existing SYNTHETIC/REAL convention ([glassBox.js](../cesium-poc/src/glassBox.js)).

Phases 0–3 total **about 8–13 days** and produce Dan's demo. A maintenance manager with finance toggled off gets 5078 → 5075 → 5079 by FIFO, or a distance order from an assumed depot. An executive who toggles finance on sees a different first site, a rank-delta explanation, and a visible "synthetic revenue" badge. Phase 2 is worth shipping on its own: it gives a real-data, explainable FIFO/distance ranking with no synthetic content at all.

Several facts conflict between sources: the TIFIA loan is reported as $603M and as $680M, Toll-by-Plate status on I-595 is unclear, and M365 rollout dates differ. No I-595-specific revenue, gantry count or outage history was found.

## Conclusion

The hard part of Dan's scenario is not the toggle UI. It is making the toggle **change the answer for a defensible reason**. Today the honest data can show two stakeholders disagreeing only if the revenue side is authored. The real assets in the data (CCTV) do not generate tolls, and FDOT, not the concessionaire, collects them. The credible version of the demo therefore makes its own provenance part of the story: real FIFO, assumed depots, synthetic revenue with ranges, and pins that no preset can override. That turns a potential credibility trap into a demonstration of the codebase's "state the gap" discipline, which few competing assistants show.

Strategically, source scoping and switchable criteria are one feature, not two. Toggling a source on adds a criterion to the ranking, and the rank-delta view is what makes a toggle visible in the result. Built with server-side enforcement and a constant tool set, the same mechanism becomes access control later, once sign-in exists. The phase that unlocks real value beyond the demo is phase 4. Per-gantry transaction and image-disposition data from FDOT/FTE is the input that turns "revenue at risk" from an illustration into a decision tool, so pursuing that data agreement early matters more than any further UI polish.
